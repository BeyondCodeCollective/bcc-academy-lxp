Opening01
The idea

The AI is not the hard part.
Deciding what it may touch is.

That is the one thing worth teaching, and the fastest way to test whether it lands is to put seven people in a room for ninety minutes and watch.

They bring nothing. Your curriculum does not change: this goes in front of it, and we merge the two once we have seen what happens.

Three things come out: where they disagreed, which moment landed, seven sentences in their own words.

The idea02
What happens · four parts
PART 01
The one nobody used

A real project that shipped perfectly and went nowhere. They guess how many people used it before we show them.

PART 02
What is in the inbox

Four real enrollment emails. No AI yet. Each person says what they would do with every one.

PART 03
Point it at them

The same four, handed to an AI with the rules the front desk uses. They vote first, then see what it decided and whether they matched.

PART 04
Your turn

Each person finishes one sentence about their own job, and it saves to their account for us to read.

01 / 04

What happens03
Part one
41
Of 11,061 eligible families

A youth center built a family portal so parents could sign up online. It shipped on time. Tests passed. No bugs. In its sixth month, 41 families used it.

The coordinator worked 146 hours on enrollment that September. In February she worked 153.

Denominator. 11,061 eligible, 423 accounts created, 41 active in the last 30 days, February 2026. Hours are first month against last; the middle dips to 138. Practice data built from the shape of a real failure.

Part four · your turn

The call I make in about four seconds that would take someone new an hour to get wrong is…

What happens to it

One sentence, in their own words. It saves to their own account, and it is the thing each person reads out loud before we close.

If someone freezes

What did you retype last week that you have retyped a hundred times?

The line to land on. Nobody in that room decided any of it on the spot — a person wrote those rules down years ago and the machine borrowed her judgment. So what is yours? Seven sentences are what you and I read on the 22nd.
