# Field Ready · Session 1 · Where AI Belongs
## The written script

Transcribed from the live session (`src/components/fde/session-stage.tsx`), so this
document and what a learner actually meets are the same thing. Ninety minutes,
four parts. Everything here is editable — change it here and in the component
together.

**The four parts** (the rail across the top of every screen)

1. **Warm up** — A short video, then a quick vocabulary game.
2. **The one nobody used** — Six months of a portal that worked perfectly.
3. **Point it at them** — Four enrollments that really arrived. Call it, then watch what it does.
4. **Your turn** — Prove the calls stuck, then write the rules that are yours.

> **Two quick rounds first.**

### Screen 5 — match the terms

**She says:** “Six words worth knowing before the story starts. Tap a term, then
tap the definition you think goes with it.”

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| System of record | The one place data is officially true — the agent never changes it without a human clicking send. |
| Guardrail | A boundary the AI can't cross by design, not by asking nicely. |
| Human in the loop | A person approves the action before it's real. |
| Happy path / unhappy path | The one clean scenario, and the thousand messy ones that are actually the job. |
| Eval | A test built from real past cases with known-correct answers — not a demo. |
| Leverage point | The smallest build that moves the most work with the least AI authority. |

Terms and definitions are both shuffled. A pair is spent once tapped, right or wrong.

### Screen 6 — quick-fire

**She says:** “Same six words, the other way around. Tap the term this definition

Same six, reversed: a definition and three terms, one question at a time, instant

## Part 2 · The one nobody used

She speaks each of these. A beat marked **[waits]** will not move on until the
learner answers — out loud, or by tapping.

**1.** “Here's the one I want you to see.”
> It went sideways at a place a lot like yours. Before I show you, I want to know one thing about you.

*She does not say hello here — the warm-up did that, ten minutes ago.*

**2. [waits]** “Has anywhere you've worked ever launched something new that almost nobody ended up using?”
> Say it out loud, or pick the closest one. There's no wrong answer here.

*They answer:* “Yes — we have one of those” · “Probably, I just never counted” · “Not that I know of”

**3.** Her reply — *depends on what they said, see below.*

**4.** “This is six months of it.”
> Poke around. Tap any month.

*Sep 58 · Oct 71 · Nov 49 · Dec 33 · Jan 46 · Feb 41 users.*

**5. [waits]** “Eleven thousand families were eligible. How many actually used it?”
> Commit to a number before you move on. Say it out loud too. That's the part that stings later.

*They commit:* About half (5,500) · A quarter (2,700) · One in ten (1,100) · One in a hundred (110)

**6.** “Not even close.”
> A quarter would have been 2,700 people. The team who built it guessed high too.

**7.** “And here's the part that gets me.”
> She was supposed to get time back. These are the coordinator's hours over the same six months.

**8.** “It worked perfectly. It just never landed.”
> Those are two different jobs, and almost nobody is assigned the second one. That second job is what we're doing today.

## Part 3 · Point it at them

### The hinge

Part 2 ends on “that second job is what we're doing today.” A held beat carries
it across, about two seconds, then the inbox arrives.

> **That second job starts here.**
> No slides for this part. Real email, real calls — yours first.

**She says:** “Four that really came in. Call each one yourself, then watch what
the AI actually did with the same rules.”

Four real enrollment emails, one tab each. For each one the learner makes the call
first — **Confirm it** (enroll them, nothing's missing) · **Hold it** (something
needs asking first) · **Give it to a person** (not a machine's call at all) — and
only then sees what the AI did. They must open all four to move on. Each reveal
also names the concept it taught.

*Footer while they're deciding:* “Call it before you look. Being wrong here is the lesson.”

### Kwame — `01_clean.txt` · Tuesday 9:04 · from Grace O.

```
Hi,

I would like to enroll my son Kwame in the after school
program at Riverbend Main. He was born 06/02/2016 and is
in 4th grade. My number is 555-0142. Signed pickup form
attached.

Thank you!
```

**It decides:** Ready to confirm
**Why:** Everything it needs is there, and nothing breaks a rule.
**It writes:** program ASP · site RB1 · grade 4 · pickup form attached
**The term:** **Clean confirm** — nothing missing, no standing rule against it.

*A learner matches it by calling:* Confirm it

---

### Marley — `02_missing_dob.txt` · Tuesday 11:20 · from unknown

```
hey can you sign up my daughter Marley for the after
school thing at eastside she is in 5th grade.
call me 555-0209

thx
```

**It decides:** Hold, ask the family
**Why:** No date of birth, so it can't check she's the right age. It drafted the reply asking for it. It didn't send it.
**It writes:** program ASP · site RB2 · grade 5 · date of birth missing
**The term:** **Hold state** — paused, not refused; missing information completes it.

*A learner matches it by calling:* Hold it

---

### Amara — `05_sibling.txt` · Wednesday 8:41 · from Grace O.

```
Sorry, one more! Can you also add Kwame's sister Amara?
She's already enrolled at Riverbend Main I think
(born 03/14/2015, grade 5). Same number, 555-0142.
Same pickup form covers both kids.
```

**It decides:** Confirm as a sibling — and hold her first day
**Why:** Same phone number as a kid already enrolled means sibling, not duplicate. That's Denise's rule, and it was written down nowhere until we wrote it down.
**The part nobody catches:** Then it caught something almost nobody in the room does. The mom says the same pickup form covers both kids. That's her assumption, not something on file. So it holds Amara's first day until a person checks that the form actually names her.
**It writes:** program ASP · site RB1 · grade 5 · flag sibling priority · first day held for check
**The term:** **Scoped confirm** — act on what's verified, hold what's only assumed.

*A learner matches it by calling:* Confirm it

---

### Theo — `08_medical.txt` · Wednesday 16:55 · from D. Osei

```
I would like to enroll Theo (DOB 2015-08-14, grade 5) in
After School at Eastside. Pickup form attached. One thing:
Theo has an inhaler he needs to keep with him and takes a
tablet at 4pm. Is there a form for that?
```

**It decides:** Refused. Straight to a human.
**Why:** Medication came up, so it won't touch this one. Not because it couldn't. Because you said anything medical goes to a person.
**The part nobody catches:** Who decided that? Not the AI. Denise did, eleven years ago, and we wrote it down. That's the job.
**It writes:** program ASP · site RB2 · flag medication · sent to a human
**The term:** **Standing rule** — a category permanently routed to a person, no matter how clear this case looks.

*A learner matches it by calling:* Give it to a person

## Part 4 · Your turn

### The hinge

Theo's reveal ends on “that's the job.” Same held beat, then the round that
checks whether any of it stuck.

> **Prove it stuck.**
> Before you write rules of your own, one quick round on theirs.

### First, the blind round

**She says:** “Four names, no story this time. Sort them — clean confirm, hold, or
a standing rule for a human. You're not reading the case again, you're testing
whether it stuck.”

The same four names, shuffled, with no case file in front of them. Each drop gets
immediate feedback with the term and its definition. It is timed.

### Then, the rulebook

**She says:** “Nobody in that room decided any of it on the spot. A person wrote
the rules down years ago and the machine borrowed her judgment. So, what are
yours?”

Three sentences to finish, one line each:

| | Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| **An automatic yes** | The call I make in about four seconds that would take someone new an hour to get wrong is… |
| **One you'd hold** | One thing at my job I'd want double-checked before it goes through — not refused, just checked — is… |
| **An automatic no** | One thing I would never let a machine touch without a person is… |

One filled prompt is enough to finish. All three save to their own account as the
session's reflection — which is where a facilitator reads them afterwards — and
finishing marks the session complete.

*Footer:* “You'll read one of these out loud.”
