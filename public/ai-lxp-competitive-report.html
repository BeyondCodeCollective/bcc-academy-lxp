02 — Verified Findings

What the Research Actually Confirmed

Three claims survived. Everything else — including most of what competitors publicly say about their own AI features — got rejected. Here's what actually held up.

3-0 Confirmed · Medium confidence

Finding 1 — AI tutoring can 2× learning gains, but only when expert-scaffolded

A Harvard RCT published June 2025 found students using an AI tutor learned roughly twice as much as a classroom control group. The setup matters though: every step of every solution was pre-authored by expert instructors. This wasn't an LLM answering questions freely. It was a tightly scripted tutor that happened to be delivered by AI.

The authors also said explicitly that the results shouldn't be generalized to community colleges, youth programs, or anything outside intro STEM courses. That's our exact context. What it tells us: a tutor locked to the curriculum and designed to ask questions instead of give answers has a real shot. A generic chatbot probably doesn't.

Kestin et al., Nature Scientific Reports, June 2025 (peer-reviewed RCT)
Education Next, "AI Tutors: Hype or Hope?" (secondary, accurately paraphrasing)
3-0 Confirmed · Medium confidence

Finding 2 — Enterprise LXP differentiation is skills-to-outcome linkage, not AI features

"AI is making people more efficient, but not necessarily more skilled. What matters isn't just access to information but systems that connect learning to skills, roles, and outcomes." — David Blake, CEO of Degreed, Vision 2025 Conference (October 2025)

That quote is confirmed and correctly attributed. It also describes exactly what enterprise HR buyers and grant funders are looking for: not how much content someone consumed, but what they can do now that they couldn't before. The platforms getting acquired are the ones that can answer that question with data.

HRTech Edge / Degreed Vision 2025 press release (primary attribution confirmed)
3-0 Confirmed · High confidence

Finding 3 — Khanmigo has no proven efficacy on test scores

Khan Academy's own blog in November 2024 said Khanmigo efficacy research was "underway" with nothing to report yet. A peer-reviewed study published in 2025 found no statistically significant difference in outcomes between Khanmigo users and a control group. All the learning gain numbers on Khan Academy's website — 20% greater than expected, 0.36 effect size — those are for the base platform, not the AI product.

Khanmigo keeps users engaged and teachers trust the brand. That's not nothing. But it means the most-cited AI tutoring success story in youth education has no outcome advantage to point to. The bar for "AI tutoring that actually works" is lower than the press would suggest, which is good news for anyone willing to measure carefully.

Khan Academy official blog, November 2024 (primary)
Journal of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 19 No. 4, 2025 (peer-reviewed)
03 — Competitive Landscape

What Competitors Are Doing
(directional — unverified vendor claims)

None of the specific claims below survived adversarial verification — meaning they're either unverifiable or overstated vendor marketing. But they describe what buyers expect to hear on a sales call, so they're worth knowing directionally.

Enterprise LXP Platforms

Degreed

Launched "Maestro," which they describe as AI-generated quizzes that adapt to learner proficiency. Also claiming a link between content and defined skill levels tied to job roles. The specific claim didn't hold up to verification, but the direction is clear: they're trying to answer "what skills does this person have now?" rather than "how many courses did they finish?" Their CEO's quote on skills-to-outcomes (confirmed in Finding 2) is the pitch they're building toward.

Docebo

Called themselves "AI-first" at Inspire 2025. Specifics from the press releases didn't hold up. The direction is the same as everyone else: content tagging, recommendations, admin automation. The differentiation claim hasn't been independently supported.

Coursera for Business / Canvas / TalentLMS / Absorb

All announced AI features over the past two years. None of the specific claims survived verification. They win on brand recognition and catalog size, not AI depth. For a large enterprise looking for something familiar, they're fine. For a funder who wants to see actual outcomes from an underrepresented community, they have nothing specific to offer.

Youth & Coding Education Platforms

Khan Academy / Khanmigo

What keeps people on Khan Academy is that it's free and teachers trust it, not Khanmigo's outcomes (see Finding 3). Khanmigo asks guiding questions rather than giving answers, which is the right instinct. Good instincts just don't equal good results, and the 2025 research bore that out.

Synthesis

Problem-solving platform for K-12 math. Strong engagement mechanics: game-like challenges, adaptive difficulty, team problem solving. Not a direct competitor — different age group, different subject, no workforce development angle.

Duolingo Max

AI explanation and roleplay features for language learning. Their personalization depends on 100M+ users' worth of data. At our scale, we can't replicate that. Honestly the streak mechanic is their real differentiator — it creates habit regardless of AI quality. Not replicable for cohort-based programs.

Sama.ai

Primarily an AI data labeling company that trains workers to annotate training data for AI systems. Not an LXP in any meaningful sense. They're building a labor supply chain for AI companies, which is a different problem entirely. Worth knowing as an example of skills-to-employment outcome storytelling done with specificity, but not a head-to-head competitor.

04 — Strategic Position

Where BCC Academy LXP Already Wins

The honest read

The AI-heavy incumbents don't have better learning data than we do. They have better marketing. We have attendance records, reflections, pre/post surveys, quiz scores, and submission quality for real students across real multi-week programs. The work is turning that into a story about what those students can actually do now.

What we have that competitors don't

Instructor-led cohorts are structurally different from self-paced content libraries. There's a reason Duolingo spent years engineering streak mechanics — they were trying to manufacture the social accountability that cohort programs have by default. We don't need to engineer it.

Most LXP platforms collect click data. We have something closer to learning evidence: reflections, submission quality, quiz performance, attendance patterns, survey sentiment. It's imperfect, but it's real behavior from real students over real time. That's harder to fake on a sales slide than engagement dashboards.

The vertical specificity matters too. Serving underrepresented communities in tech means we can make claims that a general LMS genuinely can't. That specificity is exactly what DEI budgets and mission-driven grants are designed to fund.

And practically: the Anthropic SDK is already in the codebase. We're not waiting on an infrastructure decision. Every feature in this roadmap can be built with what's already there.

05 — AI Feature Roadmap

Prioritized AI Features to Build

Ordered by how hard they are to build versus how much they change the product. Everything in Tier 1 uses the Anthropic SDK already in the codebase. Nothing here replaces instructor judgment — admins review before anything reaches students.

Tier 1 — High Impact · Low Effort (2–4 weeks each)

1. Session Recap Generator

After a recorded class, Claude reads the session description and generates a short "what we covered" card on the recording page: key concepts, main takeaways, maybe a question to sit with. Students who miss a session get context without rewatching two hours. One API call per session, no new UI needed, appends to the existing recording card.

Low effort High impact

2. AI Quiz Question Generator

The quiz system already exists. Right now questions are written by hand. Feed the week's session content to Claude, get a draft question set back, admin reviews before it publishes. Instructors still own the content — this handles the first pass, which is usually the tedious part.

Low effort High impact

3. Intake Survey Cohort Summary

When a cohort finishes intake surveys, Claude reads all the responses and generates a one-page summary: where people are starting from, what their goals are, anyone who flagged concerns. Right now admins click through every response individually. This makes the data useful before Day 1 instead of after.

Low effort High impact

4. Personalized Weekly Digest Email

A weekly email through Resend (already installed) summarizing what each student completed, what's coming up, and one line of encouragement based on their actual activity. Students feel seen. It costs almost nothing to run once it's wired up.

Low effort High impact

Tier 2 — High Impact · Medium Effort (4–8 weeks each)

5. At-Risk Learner Radar

An admin view that surfaces students who are falling behind — based on attendance patterns, missed submissions, survey sentiment shifts. Claude writes a plain-English explanation for each flag. The data already exists. This is just making it visible before it becomes a dropout instead of after.

This is the feature buyers actually care about. Not "we track completion rates." More like "we catch students before they fall off, and here's how we do it."

Med effort ★ Highest impact

6. Student Skills Profile Generator

After a student finishes a track, Claude reads their submission history, quiz performance, and reflections and produces a skills profile: what they built, what concepts they demonstrated, how their confidence shifted. Certificates are fine. This is what an employer can actually use — or what a parent can read and understand what their kid spent 8 weeks on.

Med effort ★ Highest impact

7. Scoped AI Tutor ("Ask about this week")

Not a general chatbot. A sidebar on each week's page that only knows about that week's materials. It asks questions rather than giving answers. For summer camp programs with younger students, the system prompt includes content guardrails. That combination — curriculum-locked, Socratic — is what the Harvard study points to as the version that actually works. This replaces the existing tutor button, which doesn't do much yet.

Med effort High impact

Tier 3 — Acquisition-Grade · High Effort (2–4 months each)

8. Program Impact Report Generator

At cohort close, Claude pulls together attendance rates, pre/post survey shifts, submission quality trends, certificate completions, and skills profiles and writes a funder report. BGC, grant funders, and corporate DEI partners all need this document. Right now someone has to write it by hand. Automating it means running more programs without proportionally more overhead — which is the practical definition of scaling.

High effort ★ Acquisition driver

9. Skills-to-Employment Outcomes Tracking

Three questions sent at 30, 60, and 90 days after a program ends via Resend. Did you get a job? A promotion? Start a project? Claude connects those answers back to what the student completed on the platform. "Students who finished Catalyst had a 73% job placement rate within 90 days" is the kind of number that moves a sales conversation. It's also what the Degreed CEO's confirmed quote is pointing toward — and they don't have the community-specific version of it that we could.

High effort ★ Acquisition driver

10. Multi-org White-labeling + Reporting

The architecture already supports multiple programs. The next step is making it multi-tenant: BGC runs their programs under their branding through their admin view, but it's all on our infrastructure. That's how you go from a product one org uses to a platform multiple orgs depend on. The difference in acquisition valuation between those two things is significant.

High effort ★ Acquisition driver
06 — Partnership Angle

The Black Girls Code Pitch

BGC's money comes from corporate DEI budgets and education grants. Both have reporting requirements that Canvas and Coursera can't meet with any specificity — they don't have the community-level data. We do.

What BGC funders actually ask for

Grant funders want disaggregated data: completion rates broken out by age, prior experience, geography. Our system already captures this. We just don't have a report layer sitting on top of it yet.

They want evidence of skill growth, not attendance logs. The Skills Profile (Feature #6) addresses this directly for girls ages 10-17, in language a parent can read and a funder can cite.

Any AI features need to be demonstrably age-appropriate. A scoped tutor with content guardrails (Feature #7) qualifies. Pointing to a general ChatGPT wrapper does not.

And instructor capacity is a real constraint for BGC when they're running 20+ summer camps simultaneously. The Quiz Generator and Cohort Summary reduce the per-program admin burden in ways an instructor actually feels.

07 — Recommendation

Where to Start

Start here

Cohort Summary (#3) — a day or two of work, uses data that already exists, makes admin life noticeably better before you've touched anything students see. Easy to ship, easy to demo.

At-Risk Radar (#5) — ship this before the next cohort launches. It's the one that changes what the platform is. Not a reporting feature. A signal that the platform is watching and responding, not just recording.

Every feature in the roadmap traces back to the same question: can you show that students left with more skills than they came in with? Funders don't write checks for AI features. They write checks for outcomes. Build AI that makes outcomes visible.
