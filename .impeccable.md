## Design Context

### Users

Students in Beyond Code Collective (BCC) programs — a Black-led org doing career transformation through tech training. Two audiences share this portal:

- **ATG (After The Game)** — former athletes in their 40s-50s pivoting into tech, studying for CompTIA Tech+. Many are new to computing. Adult learners juggling jobs and family.
- **The Forge** — multi-generational AI learners (digital natives + elders, often Black women) doing AI fundamentals, prompt engineering, automation.

**Context of use**: logging in between a shift, after family obligations, during a lunch break. They're making a serious career change and giving the portal real time — not casual browsing.

**Job to be done**: know where I am in the program, know what I need to do next, feel like this was built for me and the investment is real.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **earned, grounded, modern**.

- **Earned** — not flashy or hype-driven. The work being done here is hard and the UI should honor that, not decorate around it.
- **Grounded** — warm, human, Black-led. Never sterile-corporate or techbro-optimistic.
- **Modern** — clean, current, unmistakably 2026. Not retro, not skeuomorphic, not "edu-software from 2015."

**Emotional goals**: pride, seriousness-of-purpose, belonging ("this was built for me"). Not cold, not junky, not bloated, not overly solemn.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Background**: off-white — warm cream (`#F7F4EE`) or a tinted light gray. Never stark `#ffffff` (reads empty and cold) and never pure black.
- **Typography**: clean, modern, humanist sans. NOT Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, system defaults. NOT "obvious" display fonts. Geist Sans is currently in use and acceptable — any replacement must be equally unmarked / refined.
- **Serifs**: rejected for display use (tested Fraunces, felt wrong for a student portal).
- **Accent color**: restrained, single accent. User has not approved any specific accent — do not introduce rust, coral, or any hue without explicit sign-off. Never blue. Never rainbow/AI gradients.
- **Light/dark**: light mode is the default and primary experience. Dark mode is secondary.
- **Surfaces**: matte, never glossy. Cards when they earn their place — not wrapping everything.

**Anti-references** (things this must NEVER look like):
- AI slop tells: purple-to-blue gradients, neon-on-dark, gradient text on metrics, glassmorphism, sparkline decoration, big-number hero stat layouts
- Vercel template aesthetic (too clinical, too startup-demo)
- Duolingo-style gamification (confetti, mascots, cartoon rewards) — patronizing for adult career changers
- Coursera / edX / generic LMS (bloated, junky, dated)
- Generic SaaS card grids (identical card repeated 6x)
- Techbro minimalism with ironic monospace everything

### Design Principles

1. **Warm, not white.** Default surface is cream or tinted gray. Stark white is forbidden as a page background — it reads cold and templated to this audience.
2. **Restraint over decoration.** One accent color used sparingly. No motion for motion's sake. If an element isn't earning its place, cut it.
3. **Honor the work being done.** Students are mid-career-change adults doing hard academic work — typography, spacing, and hierarchy should feel as serious as the commitment they've made. Never patronize with gamification or toy-like flourishes.
4. **Built-for-me specificity.** Black-led, community-rooted. Avoid generic "inclusive stock-photo" aesthetics. The design should feel intentional and particular, not like a template that could serve any audience.
5. **No AI fingerprints.** If someone would look at this and say "an AI made this," it has failed. Avoid every pattern on the AI-slop list (gradient text, glass cards, dark-mode-with-glow, hero-metric templates, rounded icons above every heading).
