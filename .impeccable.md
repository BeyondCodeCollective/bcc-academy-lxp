## Design Context

> **The platform is white-label infrastructure, not a brand of its own.** It is the
> foundation a house is judged on — structural integrity, not the façade. We do not
> build "BCC Academy" into a marketing brand. Programs paint themselves on top through
> a constrained set of knobs; the structure underneath never changes.
>
> The design system is therefore split in two:
>
> - **The Chassis** — invariant, lives in code. Layout, type, spacing, components,
>   motion, neutral surfaces, accessibility, and the platform's *personality*. No
>   program can touch it. This is where "is it well-built" is decided.
> - **The Skin** — per-program variables, lives in data (DB-driven). One accent color,
>   a logo, optional hero imagery, name/tagline/org. A small knob set that can recolor
>   and re-energize the chassis but can never break it.
>
> "Vibrant" is delivered by the **skin** flooding bold color into defined roles. "Not
> generic" is delivered by the **chassis** having a real editorial point of view. Both
> halves are required. See `design-direction.html` for the visual target.

# PART 1 — THE CHASSIS (invariant)

### Users

Learners in Beyond Code Collective (BCC) programs and partner programs (e.g. Black
Girls Code) — career transformation through tech and AI training. The platform spans
very different audiences on the *same* chassis:

- **Adult career-changers** — e.g. former athletes (40s–50s) pivoting into tech,
  studying for CompTIA. Many new to computing. Juggling jobs and family.
- **Multi-generational AI learners** — digital natives through elders, often Black
  women, doing AI fundamentals, prompt engineering, automation.
- **Younger / playful cohorts** — e.g. the Roblox bootcamp (kid-facing, emoji icons).

**Context of use**: logging in between a shift, after family obligations, on a lunch
break. Making a serious career change and giving the portal real time — not casual
browsing.

**Job to be done**: know where I am, know what's next, feel this was built for me and
the investment is real.

### Brand personality (the chassis voice)

Three words: **earned, grounded, modern** — now sharpened to **editorial**.

- **Earned** — not flashy or hype-driven. The work is hard; the UI honors it.
- **Grounded** — warm, human, Black-led. Never sterile-corporate or techbro.
- **Modern / editorial** — clean, current, unmistakably 2026. Reads like a well-set
  publication, not "edu-software from 2015" and not a generic SaaS dashboard.

**Emotional goals**: pride, seriousness-of-purpose, belonging ("built for me").

### The affirmative direction — "Editorial Index"

The chassis has a *positive* point of view, not just a list of don'ts. It is built on
five moves. These are what make it un-generic:

1. **Visible structure over floating cards.** A real grid you can see — hairline rules,
   columns, mastheads, indices — instead of a stack of soft rounded rectangles. Content
   is *composed*, the way a print spread is, not dropped into identical tiles.
2. **Type as the hero.** A characterful display face does the heavy lifting at large,
   confident sizes with tight tracking. Headlines and numbers carry the personality.
   (Proposed: **Bricolage Grotesque** display, **Plus Jakarta Sans** or Geist Sans body.
   Current Geist Sans body is acceptable; the display face is the lever for character.)
3. **Sharp, not soft.** Square corners by default. At most a single small radius (≈2px)
   on interactive controls. **`rounded-2xl`/`rounded-xl` everywhere is the #1
   vibe-coded tell — eliminate it.** Sharpness reads as intentional and designed.
4. **High contrast, confident fields.** Replace timid grey-on-grey with bold zones:
   near-black structural bands (mastheads, footers), paper/off-white content areas, and
   the skin accent used in *large flat blocks* (a filled progress bar, a hero panel, an
   active marker) — never sprinkled pastels.
5. **Mono labels as a system.** A mono face (Geist Mono) for metadata, eyebrows, status,
   and indices — `WEEK 03`, `IN PROGRESS`, `10 SESSIONS` — used systematically and
   confidently. Numbers/indices are a recurring motif (learning is sequential: Week 01,
   Session 03, 60%). This gives a technical-but-warm, catalog-like feel.

### Surfaces

- **Background**: light neutral gray (`#f5f5f7`) or paper off-white. User tested warm
  cream (`#F7F4EE`) on 2026-06-12 and rejected it ("remove the tan"). Never pure black
  page backgrounds. Near-black (`#1a1a1a`/`#1F1B16`) is used for *structural bands*, not
  the whole page.
- **Cards**: when they earn their place — not wrapping everything. Prefer hairline-ruled
  regions and grid structure over floating soft cards. Matte, never glossy.

### Typography

Clean, modern, humanist/grotesque sans. **NOT** Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, system
defaults. Serifs rejected for display (Fraunces tested, felt wrong). The personality
lever is a confident **display** face used large; body stays unmarked and refined.

### Layout

Push past the centered single-column `max-w-5xl` default — that column is part of why it
reads generic. Allow editorial asymmetry, full-bleed structural bands, a visible grid,
and dramatic scale jumps. Dense and intentional, with generous negative space where it
counts.

### Motion

Restraint. No motion for motion's sake. Purposeful transitions only.

### Light / dark

Light mode is default and primary. Dark mode secondary.

### Accessibility (a chassis guarantee, non-negotiable)

The chassis must stay accessible **for any skin**. Because the accent is a free
per-program variable, the chassis may only use the accent in roles where contrast is
guaranteed — as a dark fill behind white text, or as an underline/marker/border where
contrast is not load-bearing. Never accent-colored text on a light surface unless the
accent is dark enough. Either constrain the skin palette or auto-derive a
contrast-correct accent. This is the rule that lets non-designers pick a color without
ever shipping something broken.

**Learner accessibility controls are required chassis features, not optional.** The
audience spans 7→77, elders, beginners, and adult career-changers, so every authenticated
surface must expose, at minimum: **adjustable text size** (a simple stepper, persists
across the session) and **read-aloud / text-to-speech** of the learning content. These
live in the chassis and are available on every program/skin. Treat them as first-class,
visible affordances — not buried in settings.

### Anti-references (must NEVER look like)

- AI-slop tells: purple-to-blue gradients, neon-on-dark, gradient text on metrics,
  glassmorphism, sparkline decoration, big-number hero-stat templates, **a soft rounded
  card repeated 6×**, rounded icon-chip above every heading.
- Vercel-template clinical startup-demo aesthetic.
- Duolingo gamification (confetti, mascots, cartoon rewards) — patronizing for adult
  career-changers.
- Coursera / edX / generic LMS (bloated, junky, dated).
- Techbro minimalism with ironic monospace *everything* (mono is a *labeling system*
  here, used with intent — not the whole UI).

### Design principles

1. **Warm, not white.** Default surface is tinted gray/paper. Stark white as a full page
   background reads cold and templated.
2. **Restraint over decoration.** One accent, used boldly but sparingly. Cut anything
   not earning its place.
3. **Honor the work.** Typography, spacing, and hierarchy should feel as serious as the
   commitment the learner has made. Never patronize with toy flourishes.
4. **Built-for-me specificity.** Black-led, community-rooted, particular — not generic
   "inclusive stock-photo" templating.
5. **No AI fingerprints.** If someone would say "an AI made this," it failed.

# PART 2 — THE SKIN (per-program variables)

Everything below is **data**, set per program (ultimately in the DB, editable by admins
without a deploy). The token plumbing already exists in `globals.css`
(`--accent`, `bg-accent`, `text-accent`, `border-rule`, `bg-surface-elevated`,
`bg-muted-bg`, `text-ink`) and in `ProgramConfig.colors`/`logo`. Today every program
fills these with the *same* cobalt-and-charcoal values — the engine exists but nothing
varies. The work is making the skin actually differ per program.

### The knob set (keep it tiny)

| Token | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **accent** | the program's one brand color | author picks ONE value; system derives hover + tint |
| **logo / wordmark** | program or co-sign mark | swappable per program |
| **hero imagery** | optional program illustration/photo | optional; rides the chassis frame |
| **name / tagline / organization** | program identity text | already in `ProgramConfig` |

That is the *entire* surface area exposed to a program. No spacing, type, radius, or
layout control — those belong to the chassis.

### How the skin creates "vibrant"

The accent **floods defined roles**, it does not sprinkle: the hero panel fill, the
progress-bar fill, the active-week marker, link underlines, the primary action. Same
chassis bones, different energy:

- A playful program (Roblox) sets an electric accent → the chassis feels alive and fun.
- A serious program (CompTIA Security+) sets a sharp professional accent → the same
  chassis feels rigorous.
- A one-pager / landing page sets its accent + a hero image and inherits the whole
  chassis by setting a few CSS variables — no rebuild. The system must reach past the
  LMS to marketing one-pagers and landing pages, not just the dashboard.

### Co-sign / "powered by" rule

The platform is **fully invisible inside the product** — the app chrome shows the
*program's* identity only (e.g. "Upskill Bahamas"). Do **not** put "powered by BCC" or
any BCC co-sign in the product UI; the user explicitly removed it (2026-06-13). BCC
appears only where legally/technically unavoidable (domain, email sender, legal pages).

Where a co-sign *is* used (e.g. partner-program marketing), it points at **orgs we
control** (BGC / BCC), never at third-party content or credential brands. "Powered by
Roblox" / "powered by CompTIA" implies an endorsement or partnership we may not have.
Say "Roblox bootcamp" or "CompTIA Tech+ prep" — describe the subject, don't borrow the
trademark's authority.

### Notes

- **Cobalt `#1D59FF` is no longer "the brand accent."** It is *Catalyst's default skin
  value*. Other programs set their own. (Dashboard blue is still fine where the user has
  approved it.)
- Per-program subdomains require an IT/DNS request — programs currently co-exist on
  `bccacademy.io`, carried by the program cookie. Skin is per-program regardless of host.
